What about winning the fight? Options in combat: Attack, Guard, Item, and Persona. Any of these use different in-universe methods to accomplish much the same things, which are: - `.attack` - `.heal` - `.defend` Each Persona `.skill` has an SP cost, but I'll get back to that. The basic concept is: if enough subsequent `.attack` details are open in one `.battle`, you win that battle. Selecting 'Persona' brings up your current Persona(based on what you used last, if you didn't refresh the page)'s skills, from which you can navigate to the persona submenu, where each Persona is a `details` that can be clicked to bring up its skills. You navigate to the submenu by closing the current Persona's `details`, so you can only have one Persona `details` open at a time. Based on the Persona you have active, you'll take more damage from attacks with affinities the Persona is weak to. You'll take less damage while guarding. You'll recover health (or recover your teammates' health) by healing. The enemy will take more damage from attacks with affinities they're weak to. Your teammates attack by themselves: each time you select your action for that turn, it's a `details` with an `.expand_all` class, and your teammates' actions are invisible `details` with the `.expand` class, grouped with your action. Then, by tracking how many `.attack`s were used this way, you can determine the enemy's health. `.attacks` will also have the affinity(or just a more general `.supereffective`) attached, to track if any of the attacks did more damage than expected.
